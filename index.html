ABOUT ME

Understanding extremes.
Improving the way we model them.

I am a stochastic hydrologist and a recipient of the Prime Minister’s Research Fellowship (PMRF). I completed my PhD in Hydraulics and Water Resources Engineering at Indian Institute of Technology (BHU), Varanasi, India, under the supervision of Prof. K. K. Pandey.

My research focuses on process-informed statistical models for hydroclimatic extremes. During my PhD, I examined how uncertainties arising from threshold selection and climatic-covariate selection can be reduced in nonstationary extreme-value analysis. This led to a framework that represents rainfall occurrence and severity separately, allowing their distinct climatic controls and contributions to design return levels to be examined.

My future research aims to develop physically informed nonlinear relationships that remain robust under changing climatic conditions, and to extend this decomposition philosophy from rainfall to flood-generating processes.

RESEARCH

From processes to probabilities

Three connected directions
01

How often, and how intense?

Representing the occurrence and severity of rainfall extremes separately within a nonstationary Poisson–GPD framework, and examining their combined influence on design rainfall return levels.

Extreme-value theoryOccurrence–severity decomposition
02

Reducing uncertainty in modelling choices

Developing diagnostic approaches for threshold selection and climatic-covariate selection in nonstationary extreme-value analysis, with the aim of improving the reliability and interpretability of design return levels.

Diagnostic modellingAtmospheric covariates
03

Connecting rainfall to flood risk

My future work aims to learn physically informed nonlinear relationships under changing climatic conditions and extend the occurrence–severity decomposition from rainfall to flood-generating processes, building on experience in hydrological and hydraulic modelling.
